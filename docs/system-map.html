Why capable graduates stay invisible

Yaseen Selemane · 28 October 2026 · system map and causal analysis
Employers don’t lack information about graduates. They’re drowning in it. So stop trying to prove the graduate is good — make being wrong about a graduate cheap.

The experiment South Africa already ran

33.6%official unemployment
47.4%youth 15–34
62.8%youth 15–24
12.4%graduate — was 10.3% two quarters ago

Stats SA, Quarterly Labour Force Survey Q2 2026, released 11 August 2026.

South Africa already pays employers to hire young people. The Employment Tax Incentive cuts an employer’s PAYE bill for workers aged 18–29, claimed monthly through the EMP201 return, without touching the employee’s wage.

The state already made it cheaper to take a chance on a young person. Graduate unemployment went up anyway. So money was never the whole constraint — the employer still cannot tell who is capable.

The incentive de-risked the cost of a wrong hire. It did not de-risk the judgement. That gap is what the rest of this page maps, and what the intervention fills.

What the kit actually is

A real job ad goes in. Out comes the three observable behaviours that predict success in that role with proxy requirements stripped out, a 45-minute work sample that tests those three and nothing else, and an anchored rubric a human fills in.

The tool drafts. A person scores and decides. That line is a design position, not an omission, and it is where the fairness argument lives.
